Simple controls · Predictable results

Learn it by ear.

Each control always changes the same part of Icy Beauty. This guide describes what you will hear, where the useful ranges sit, and which controls work well together.

Start here

Hold a four-note chord and move one parameter at a time. Return it to 50% before trying the next control; Release defaults to 65%.

Colour

Tone

Tone changes both brightness and the balance of the three tone layers. The rounded main note stays present; raising Tone brings up both the near-octave and high-interval layers, with the high interval growing more strongly, and reduces smoothing.

  • Low: soft basses, rounded leads, and woody chords.
  • Middle: the balanced factory sound.
  • High: bright bells and glass-like leads.

Pair it with Resonance: Tone supplies harmonic detail and Resonance turns that detail into a clearer ring.

Slow movement

Motion

Every active voice receives its own slow pitch movement. Voices do not move together, so a held chord gently spreads and closes. This is pitch movement, not a chorus or delay effect. The values below assume the modulation wheel and pressure are at zero.

0%No slow pitch movement
25%Very gentle drift
50%About ±6.5 cents around 0.54 Hz
75%Clearly animated ensemble movement
100%About ±13 cents around 1 Hz

The modulation wheel raises Motion toward maximum. Aftertouch adds a smaller amount.

Fast texture

Grain

Grain combines tiny random pitch changes with a noise layer. The first half stays subtle; the second half moves more quickly toward rough, weathered sounds. At 100%, the random pitch part is about ±2.6 cents.

  • Motion is slow and smooth.
  • Grain is fast and uneven.

Low Tone plus Grain sounds breathy and worn. High Tone plus Grain makes the bright layers feel brittle.

Tuned ring

Resonance

Resonance mixes in a pitched ring that follows every played note. It stays connected to the note rather than running away into self-oscillation. The ranges below assume pressure is at zero; aftertouch can raise Resonance above the panel setting.

  • Below 30%: a small outline around the note.
  • Around 50%: the factory sound’s clear icy edge.
  • Above 70%: an obvious glass-like ring.

Polyphonic aftertouch raises Resonance on one note. Channel pressure raises it on every active note on that MIDI channel.

Fade time

Release

Release sets how long a note fades after key-up or gate-low. The lower half gives precise short and medium values; the upper half opens into long tails.

Higher Resonance usually makes a long Release easier to hear.

Polyphony

Voices

Choose one to sixteen voices when adding the algorithm. The default is eight. MIDI and CV share this pool, and output gain is adjusted so adding voices does not cause a large level jump.

1Bass, melody, or external arpeggiation
2Intervals and duophonic lines
4Compact chords
6Extended chords
8The balanced default
12Dense chords with several CV voices
16Maximum MIDI or CV polyphony

Polyphonic control

CV/Gate groups

Choose zero to six gate groups when adding the algorithm; the default is four. Every Gate input starts at None and every Count starts at 0, leaving all voices available to MIDI until CV is explicitly connected.

Each group’s pitch buses immediately follow its selected gate. Gate on Input 9 with Count 3 reads pitches from Inputs 10, 11, and 12. Count is limited to 11, the voices left after the other groups' reservations, and the available bus positions after the selected gate bus.

Gates rise above 1.0 V and fall below 0.5 V. Sample & hold Off tracks pitch while high; On captures pitch at the rising edge. Increasing Count under a held gate waits for the next edge, while decreasing it fades the removed voices with an internal 5 ms transition, independent of Release. A retrigger during an audible release tail resumes smoothly.

Gate groups reserve fixed voice partitions in group order. MIDI uses the unreserved remainder. Incoming MIDI and CV notes do not steal from the other partition, but editing Counts can reallocate the pool and quickly fade notes that no longer fit.

Pitch follows 1 V/octave with 0 V at MIDI note 48 (about 130.81 Hz). At 48 kHz, calibration is verified from −5 V through +7 V to within 0.01 cent. Pitch CV is clamped to the −8 V to +8 V range, and extreme positive values are also limited below Nyquist, so the usable top end is lower at reduced sample rates.

Global transpose

Pitch offsets

The Setup page’s Semitones and Octaves controls transpose every MIDI and CV voice. Semitones covers −11 to +11; Octaves covers −4 to +4. Both default to zero and update held notes immediately.

Sample & hold continues to hold the sampled input CV while either global pitch offset can still be changed.
